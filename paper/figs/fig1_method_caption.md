**A decision language model reads a text state and returns, in one forward pass, a distribution over a fixed gene panel; every method is scored with the same likelihood and sampler.** (1) The state is a prompt: a cell type (conditional generation) or a perturbation together with a random control cell $x_0$, written as its 48 most abundant panel genes with quantized log-mass levels (the tokens shown are the first ones of a real K562 control cell). (2) A LoRA-tuned language model encodes the prompt; a linear decision head on the last-token state, with an optional fixed log-prior $b$ (none, the training mean, or the smoothed control cell itself), gives one logit per gene, and a softmax gives $q$ on the simplex. (3) A real cell is the probability vector $p=x/N$ of its counts (or a transformed version); training minimizes the soft cross-entropy between $p$ and $q$ (dashed). (4) A shared sampler draws a generated cell from $q$ with a training library size, and held-out real cells of unseen donors or perturbations score the model by likelihood, sample quality and perturbation effects. Colours: teal conditioning, purple model, blue data, orange training and evaluation only. Bar heights are schematic.
